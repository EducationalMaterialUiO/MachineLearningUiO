=== 1. the explicit construction of Theorem 8.relurate ===
  A single hidden layer of N ReLU units, weights written down rather
  than trained: interpolate F at the N+1 knots t_k = a + k(b-a)/N.
  The bound is L(b-a)/2N.

  target F(x) = sin(2 pi x) on [0,1], Lipschitz constant L = 2 pi

=== 2. representability is not learnability ===
  Same architecture, same width, but the weights are found by Adam
  from a random start rather than written down.  Best of five seeds,
  6000 full-batch steps on 2001 points, lr 0.01 with cosine decay.

     N   constructed   default init   kinks spread   ratio
     2     1.000e+00      1.000e+00      9.170e-01     0.9
     4     2.105e-01      1.108e+00      9.170e-01     4.4
     8     7.038e-02      1.837e-01      8.465e-02     1.2
    16     1.885e-02      7.203e-02      5.557e-02     2.9
    32     4.792e-03      4.279e-02      1.660e-02     3.5
    64     1.203e-03      4.386e-02      6.359e-03     5.3
  the last column is the trained-with-spread-kinks error divided by
  the constructed error; best of five seeds in each column
  the default initialisation is the worse problem: most kinks start
  outside [0,1] and never enter it.  Even with the kinks placed where
  the construction puts them, gradient descent falls further behind
  the constructed network as the width grows.  The existence proof
  says nothing whatever about the optimisation problem.

=== 3. depth against width: the sawtooth s_k ===
  T(x) = 2 relu(x) - 4 relu(x - 1/2) is the tent map, computed by one
  layer of two ReLU units.  s_k = T composed k times has 2^k linear
  pieces, so a depth-k network with 2k units in total represents it
  exactly, while one hidden layer needs at least 2^k - 1 units.

     k   units in the deep net   linear pieces measured   2^k
     1                       2                        2      2
     2                       4                        4      4
     3                       6                        8      8
     4                       8                       16     16
     5                      10                       32     32
     6                      12                       64     64
     7                      14                      128    128
     8                      16                      256    256
  the deep network is exact and its size grows linearly in k while
  the number of pieces it produces grows exponentially

  Now the shallow networks.  For each k we fit one hidden layer of
  width N to s_k and measure the sup error and the number of linear
  pieces the fitted network actually has.

=== 4. Barron's rate, Theorem 8.barron ===
  Target F(x) = (1/M) sum_m c_m cos(kappa a_m . x), M = 12 unit ridge
  directions a_m drawn at random.  Its Fourier transform is a sum of
  point masses at +-kappa a_m, so the Barron norm of Eq. (8.barronnorm)
  is known exactly:  C_F = kappa (1/M) sum_m |c_m|.  Crucially C_F does
  not depend on d, and we draw x uniformly from the unit ball so that
  the radius r = 1 in every dimension too.  Theorem 8.barron then
  predicts the same bound (2 r C_F)^2 / N in d = 1, 5 and 20.

  kappa = 2:  C_F = 1.7281

      8   5.329e-07   6.819e-05   2.752e-04           1.493e+00
     16   2.202e-07   5.162e-05   6.524e-05           7.466e-01
     32   2.785e-07   8.314e-06   2.814e-05           3.733e-01
     64   4.307e-07   4.529e-06   2.267e-05           1.866e-01
    128   3.776e-07   4.238e-06   1.553e-05           9.332e-02
  measured slopes of log(mse) against log(N):   -0.53  -1.23  -1.02   (Theorem 8.barron: -1)
  ratio of the d = 20 error to the d = 1 error at N = 128:  41.1

      4   1.296e-02   2.689e-02   3.186e-02           4.778e+01

     32   2.491e-04   1.391e-03   4.978e-04           5.973e+00
     64   2.693e-04   1.247e-03   6.183e-04           2.986e+00
    128   1.850e-04   7.520e-04   6.779e-04           1.493e+00

  ratio of the d = 20 error to the d = 1 error at N = 128:  3.7

  the bound of Theorem 8.barron holds everywhere and with room to
  spare; the error grows with kappa, that is with C_F, and only
  weakly with d.  Compare Eq. (8.curse): for a general Lipschitz
  target the width needed at fixed accuracy is raised to the power d,
  and no experiment at d = 20 would be possible at all
